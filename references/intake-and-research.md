# Intake and research

## Choose the claim level first

| Product | Permitted framing | Do not claim |
|---|---|---|
| Fandom or character match | “Which travel role/archetype feels most like your response pattern?” | the participant or source person has a diagnosed personality type |
| Brand archetype quiz | preference, work style, or campaign role | hiring fitness, mental health, or a stable identity |
| Self-reflection quiz | “your current tendency in this scenario” | clinical traits, trauma, attachment diagnosis, IQ, or treatment advice |
| Authorized psychometric instrument | the instrument's stated scope | any inference beyond its manual/licence/validation population |

Write the disclaimer in plain language. It should be visible but not used to excuse unsupported scoring.

## Source lanes

Use the lane that can support the requested topic; do not flatten them into one evidence bucket.

1. **Primary corpus:** official material, original episodes/books/films/games, creator-approved material, first-party transcripts, supplied research, or observable product behavior.
2. **Interpretive context:** credible interviews, authorized commentary, reviews, community discussion. Useful for language and candidate scenes, not proof on their own.
3. **Psychology/method:** original scale manuals, peer-reviewed measures, professional associations, or validated public instruments. Use for construct vocabulary and question-quality checks, not to diagnose celebrities or fictional characters.
4. **Audience voice:** user briefs, comments, focus groups, and community language. Useful for tone, meme references, and recognition; label it as audience framing.

For web research, search primary/official sources first. Use communities and social platforms to find repeated audience friction or terms, then return to primary sources before treating a claim as factual. Keep a ledger with URL/source, date, lane, supported claim, and uncertainty.

## From source to question

For every candidate question, record:

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Context | A concrete situation the target audience recognizes or can imagine |
| Construct | One observable tension, e.g. directness vs mediation, task action vs emotional repair |
| Answer task | Forced choice, scenario options, agreement scale, or frequency scale; use the wording that matches the stem |
| Contrast | Why answers would separate outcomes rather than merely sound nicer/worse |
| Weights | A vector per answer, with a human explanation |
| Sensitivity | Whether the topic could be coercive, defamatory, discriminatory, medical, or personally identifying |

Avoid double-barrelled stems, moralized options, “all of the above” answers, and questions that only restate a result label. A five-point scale should use wording that matches the proposition: agreement for a belief, frequency for a habit, likelihood for a future action, or resemblance for a role match.

## User approvals

Ask the user to approve separately:

1. source boundary and fictional/real-person framing;
2. result types and their short/long copy;
3. final question text and answers;
4. named assets, music, and rights;
5. analytics/deployment consent.

Do not quietly substitute model-written text for user-approved copy. Preserve editable source text in the config and keep rejected variants out of release copy.
